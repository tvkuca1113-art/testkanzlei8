Who this is for

Munich attracts people from everywhere, and the German tax system does not make it easy for them. Most of the questions we hear come from the same handful of situations:

  • You moved to Germany for work and face your first German tax return.
  • Part of your pay arrives as RSUs, stock options or an ESPP, and nobody explained how that is taxed here.
  • You are leaving Germany, or arriving mid-year, and no one has told you which income belongs where.
  • You want to work as a freelancer and need to know whether you are Freiberufler or Gewerbe, and what follows from that.
  • You still have property, accounts or an inheritance abroad and are unsure what Germany expects you to declare.

None of these are exotic. They are simply badly served in English, because the answers sit in German statutes and German case law.

One thing to be clear about

Advice, meetings and explanations happen in English. Filings and official correspondence are in German — that is a statutory requirement, not a preference. The firm handles that side for you and explains what each letter actually says.

Your first German tax return

For employees, filing is often voluntary — and often worth it, because wage tax is withheld on a monthly basis without knowing your full year. Deductions that regularly get missed include commuting costs, a home office, relocation expenses, double household costs while your family is still abroad, professional training, and church tax matters.

Filing becomes mandatory in a number of cases, among them additional income above the exemption limit, certain tax class combinations for married couples, receipt of wage replacement benefits such as Elterngeld or Kurzarbeitergeld, and — importantly for internationals — foreign income. Which case applies is the first thing we establish.

RSUs, options and equity compensation

This is where the largest amounts go wrong. In Germany, equity compensation is generally employment income, taxed when it vests or when an option is exercised — not when you sell. The later sale is a separate capital gains question with its own rules.

It gets harder when the vesting period spans two countries. The income then has to be apportioned across the working days in each state, and the applicable double taxation treaty decides who may tax which slice. Employers frequently report the full amount in one country, which leaves the employee to correct it.

What we do: reconstruct the vesting timeline, apportion correctly, apply the treaty, claim credit or exemption where it is due, and document it so it survives a query from the tax office.

Arriving in and leaving Germany

You become subject to unlimited German tax liability once you have a residence under section 8 of the Fiscal Code (Abgabenordnung) or a habitual abode under section 9. Neither depends on your registration at the Bürgeramt — keeping and using a flat is enough. From that point, your worldwide income is in scope, even if a treaty later exempts part of it.

Leaving is not neutral either. If you hold a substantial shareholding in a company, giving up German residence can trigger exit taxation under section 6 of the Foreign Tax Act: the unrealised gain is taxed as if you had sold, without any money changing hands. Relief exists within the EU and EEA but comes with conditions. This has to be looked at before you move, because afterwards the sequence is fixed.

Going freelance in Germany

The first decision is the one people skip: are you a Freiberufler — a member of one of the liberal professions — or are you running a Gewerbe? The answer determines whether you owe trade tax, whether you must register with the trade office, whether you may use simple cash accounting, and how the tax office will treat you for years.

  • Registration with the tax office and your tax number
  • VAT: standard taxation or the small business rule, and what each means for your invoices
  • Whether an EU VAT identification number makes sense for you
  • Advance payments, so the first assessment does not arrive as a shock
  • Bookkeeping that satisfies German record-keeping standards from day one

Income and assets abroad

Rental property in your home country, dividends from a foreign broker, a pension from two systems, an inheritance from your parents. Under unlimited liability these belong in the German return — even when the treaty gives the taxing right to the other state. In that case Germany exempts the income but still uses it to set the rate on everything else. That mechanism is the single most overlooked item we see.

Assume the information reaches the German tax office regardless: account data is exchanged automatically between more than a hundred jurisdictions. Volunteering complete figures is considerably cheaper than answering a query later.

If you are a US person

We cover the German side in full — German returns, treaty application, German reporting duties. We do not prepare US federal returns, FBAR or FATCA filings; that requires a US preparer. We work alongside one regularly and supply the German figures they need. Saying this plainly is better than discovering it in April.
